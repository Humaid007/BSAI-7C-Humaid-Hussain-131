**Panels and Week 2 channel rules.** **1, LOS histogram:** asks how patient length of stay is distributed; a histogram suits a continuous measure and reveals right skew. Position along a common quantitative axis and binning encode frequency; bin width is **0.5 day**. **2, ward LOS boxplots:** asks whether LOS differs among wards; boxplots compare medians and spread across categories. Ward is encoded by x-position and LOS by a common y-position/scale, not colour alone; ward order is General Medicine, Cardiology, Day Surgery, Intensive Care. **3, systolic–diastolic scatter:** asks whether the two blood-pressure measures co-vary; a scatterplot exposes association and outliers. Both variables use quantitative position on labelled axes in **mmHg**; points use transparency rather than extra category colours. **4, correlation heatmap:** asks which cleaned measures are associated; a heatmap compactly compares pairwise Pearson correlations. A diverging palette is centred at **r = 0**, bounded **−1 to +1**, with every cell annotated numerically so colour is not the only channel. MAP is excluded because it is derived from systolic/diastolic pressure.

**Parameters defended.** Figure size **15×10 in** and `constrained_layout=True` keep four panels and labels legible. Histogram `binwidth=0.5` day balances detail and readability and is declared on the axis. Boxplot ward order is fixed for consistent comparison; `showfliers=False` is only a display choice, **not** data cleaning, and extreme LOS records are separately flagged/excluded under the stated >60-day rule. Scatter size **18** and alpha **0.35** reduce overplotting; title reports Pearson **r**. Heatmap uses Pearson correlation, `center=0`, `vmin=-1`, `vmax=1`, `annot=True`, `fmt='.2f'`, `square=True`, `linewidths=.5`, and 7-point annotations for comparability/readability. Palette comes from `vizlib.PALETTE`; top/right spines are removed; labels state units; source note names the input and cleaning rules. The named `fig` is saved **before** closing at **300 dpi** with `bbox_inches='tight'`.
